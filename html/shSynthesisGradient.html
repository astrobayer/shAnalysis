Spatial

shLowLevel.shSynthesisGradient

North, east and up gradient of any synthesized quantity — the vector field to draw over a scalar map, or to advect tracers through.

Syntax

[dq, info] = shLowLevel.shSynthesisGradient(C, S, GM, R, lat, lon, ...
    Quantity="ewh", kn=kn)                          % grid, per metre
dq = g.gradient(lat, lon, Quantity="ewh", kn=kn, Units="perRadian")
dq = g.gradient(latP, lonP, Quantity="geoid", Mode="points")
dq = g.gradient(lat, lon, Quantity="potential", Height=250e3)

Description

Returns the spatial gradient of the field shLowLevel.shSynthesis would produce from the SAME coefficients under the SAME options, resolved in the local north-east-up frame:

dq.north = (1/r) ∂Q/∂φ              = (1/r) Σ fₙ Σ dPbarₙₘ/dφ (...)
dq.east  = (1/(r cosφ)) ∂Q/∂λ      = (1/(r cosφ)) Σ fₙ Σ m Pbarₙₘ (...)
dq.up    = ∂Q/∂r                  = -(1/r) Σ pₙ fₙ Σ Pbarₙₘ (...)

with (...) = Cₙₘ cos(mλ) + Sₙₘ sin(mλ), fₙ the degree factor from shLowLevel.kernelFactors and pₙ its radial exponent. The horizontal components use the exact dPbar/dφ of shLowLevel.legendreALFDeriv (frozen validated identity).

The contract, and why it is stated that way

The declared contract is exact derivative of shSynthesis under identical options. The defaults therefore match shSynthesis (Quantity="geoid", Nmin=0), and the correctness test differentiates shSynthesis itself rather than a second model of it. A gradient that quietly used a different kernel, truncation or mean field than the scalar map it is drawn over would be arrows over the wrong picture, and no other test would notice.

Which quantities have an up component

∂Q/∂r exists exactly for the quantities that carry an upward-continuation law — i.e. exactly those that accept Height:

Quantitydq.upRadial exponent pₙ
potentialfieldn+1
gravity_anomaly, gravity_disturbancefieldn+2
gravity_gradient_rrfieldn+3
geoid, ewh, surface_density, bottom_pressure, deformation_up, none[] (empty)none — surface concept
An equivalent water height one kilometre up is not a smaller equivalent water height, it is nothing. The up component is returned EMPTY rather than as zeros or NaN: empty fails loudly at the first use, a NaN field would be plotted. info.hasUp is the programmatic form of the same fact, and Height is refused for the same quantities for the same reason.

Not the same as deformation

shLowLevel.shSynthesisDeformation's horizontal components are NOT this gradient. Elastic horizontal displacement carries the load Love number l'ₙ and the vertical one carries h'ₙ, so the two fields differ by a degree-dependent factor, not by a constant. Use this function for “which way does the field fall”, that one for “which way does the crust move”. Deformation is a vector because of l'ₙ; every other quantity is a scalar that has a gradient.

Vector overlays and particle animation

The intended consumer is a vector overlay or a tracer advection drawn over a scalar map. Three points decide whether that is honest rather than decorative:

  • Advect in geographic space, project only to draw. Stepping a tracer in projected coordinates makes its speed wrong by the local scale factor. With Units="perRadian" the components are angular rates of change of Q, so an angular mobility μ gives dφ/dt = μ·dq.north with no metric factor left to remember; with Units="perMeter" a linear mobility gives a velocity v = μ·dq.north in m/s and dφ/dt = v/r. (The first printing of this page put the /r on the perRadian form, which belongs to the perMeter one.)
  • Grid and interpolate, do not evaluate per tracer per frame. Mode="points" pays a Legendre recursion per point; it is for when the exact value at a station matters, not for 10⁴ tracers at 30 fps.
  • A tracer image shows direction and relative speed, never magnitude. Nothing in a flowing-tracer picture carries a number, so it must be drawn over a scalar field that does — never instead of one.

Validation

Python-validated before implementation (tools/dev/validate_gradient.py): all three components against 5-point finite differences of an independent scalar synthesis at 1e-12 (the difference floor); the identities ∂(potential)/∂r = −gravity_disturbance and ∂(gravity_disturbance)/∂r = −gravity_gradient_rr at 4e-16, which reduce the up component to kernels the toolbox already validates; single-coefficient closed forms at 2e-16. In MATLAB, testCorrectness repeats all three against shSynthesis itself.
