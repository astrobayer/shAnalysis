shLowLevel.leakageDataDriven

Model-free leakage/damage correction of a catchment mean.

Syntax

[fHat, info] = shLowLevel.leakageDataDriven(gridSeries, latDeg, lonDeg)[fHat, info] = shLowLevel.leakageDataDriven(gridSeries, latDeg, lonDeg, Mask=[], Filter="gauss300", Nmax=NaN, ...)

Description

FHAT = shLowLevel.leakageDataDriven(GRIDSERIES, LATDEG, LONDEG, Mask = R, Filter = F) repairs the catchment average of a FILTERED time series using correction terms built from the filtered fields alone - Vishwakarma's "method of deviation". No hydrological model is involved, which is the whole point: it is the model-independent counterpart of shLowLevel.leakageCorrect's forward model.

Examples

From the help block
    % ewh is (nLat x nLon x T) of Gaussian-300 filtered fields
    [fHat, info] = shLowLevel.leakageDataDriven(ewh, lat, lon, ...
        Mask = basin, Filter = "gauss300");
    fprintf("leakage removed: %.3f m RMS, phase %.1f deg\n", ...
        rms(info.leakage), info.phaseDeg);

  References

      damage due to filtering of mass change estimates from the GRACE
      satellite mission, PhD thesis, Institute of Geodesy, University
      of Stuttgart - chapter 2, eqs. (2.22)-(2.28) for the scale
      factor, and chapter 3, eqs. (3.19)-(3.29).
    Vishwakarma, Horwath, Devaraju, Groh & Sneeuw (2017), A data-driven
      approach for repairing the hydrological catchment signal damage
      due to filtering of GRACE products, Water Resour. Res. 53,
      9824-9844, doi:10.1002/2017WR021150.
    Vishwakarma, Devaraju & Sneeuw (2016), Water Resour. Res. 52,
      doi:10.1002/2016WR018960 - the method of scale.
    Phillips, Nerem, Fox-Kemper, Famiglietti & Rajagopalan (2012), The
      influence of ENSO on global terrestrial water storage using
      GRACE, Geophys. Res. Lett. 39, L16705, doi:10.1029/2012GL052495
      - the Hilbert phase estimator.
    Python-validated in tools/dev/validate_leakage_datadriven.py: the
      decomposition closes to 4e-15, the phase measure/shift pair are
      mutual inverses to 7e-15 deg, and a closed loop removes 63% of
      the filtering damage. testCorrectness measures 55.3% on its own
      fixture; the grids and the basin differ, the method does not.

  shLowLevel.gridScaling, shLowLevel.basinKernel.

Name-Value Arguments

NameSizeTypeDefaultDescription
Maskn x mdouble[](nLat x nLon) double the catchment function R: 1 inside, 0 outside. Fractional edge values are allowed and are used as given. The default is empty and the function then refuses: there is no sensible default catchment, so this is required in practice

Error Identifiers

IdentifierRaised when
shLowLevel:leakageDataDriven:badFilterFilter is refused by shLowLevel.filterSpec: it is not a W struct, "none", "gauss<radiusKm>" or "DDK1" to "DDK8"
shLowLevel:leakageDataDriven:badMaskMask is missing, or is not nLat x nLon
shLowLevel:leakageDataDriven:badNmaxthe grid supports nmax < 2; give at least 3 latitudes
shLowLevel:leakageDataDriven:badSizegridSeries is not nLat x nLon x T for the given latDeg/lonDeg
shLowLevel:leakageDataDriven:emptyMaskMask has zero area - there is no catchment to average over
shLowLevel:leakageDataDriven:nanInputgridSeries contains a non-finite value; the phase and ratio steps are least squares over the WHOLE series and cannot skip one epoch, so this refuses rather than return NaN
shLowLevel:leakageDataDriven:tooFewEpochsfewer than 12 epochs. The phase and amplitude steps are regressions over the time series, and below one annual cycle they fit noise
