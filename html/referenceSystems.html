Normal field & GM/R rescaling (v2.4)

Subtracting a WGS84/GRS80 normal field and converting between (GM, R) conventions.

shLowLevel.rescaleGMR / g.toReference

g2 = g.toReference(GM=3.986004418e14, R=6378137.0);
% C2_nm = C_nm (GM1/GM2)(R1/R2)^n - the physical field is invariant

Needed whenever conventions meet: the WGS84 GM (3.986004418e14) and a (6378137.0) DIFFER from the ICGEM values (3.986004415e14, 6378136.3); coefficient-wise mixing without rescaling costs millimetres. The arithmetic operators guard against it (shCoefficients:constantsMismatch).

shLowLevel.normalFieldCS / g.subtractNormalField

T = g.subtractNormalField();          % WGS84 (default) | System="GRS80"
T.map(lat, lon, Quantity="geoid")    % proper +-100 m undulations, drawn by geoMap

The even zonals are COMPUTED from the defining constants (GM, a, f, ω) via the Heiskanen-Moritz closed form (q₀ series → J₂ → J₂ₖ) — no coefficient tables; validated against NIMA TR8350.2 to all published digits (J₂ = 1.082629821313e-3, Cbar₂₀/₄₀/₆₀/₈₀). subtractNormalField rescales the ellipsoid field to the model's (GM, R) automatically, including the degree-0 GM-ratio term.

Permanent tide. The ellipsoid is tide-free by construction; your model's C20 carries its own convention (ICGEM header 'tide_system'; zero-tide vs tide-free differ by ~4.2e-9 in Cbar20). The toolbox does not convert silently — check the header.

Pole-tide conventions: C21/S21 v2.7.0

Processing centers remove the (solid + ocean) pole tide using a mean pole model, and the model is a convention, not a measurement. The IERS 2010 conventional model (cubic until 2010.0, linear after) and the 2018 secular-pole update (linear, adopted for GRACE/GRACE-FO RL06) differ by up to ~1e-10 in C21/S21 — with a strong trend component. Mixing conventions across solutions therefore biases C21/S21 trend studies at the mm-EWH level, silently. shLowLevel.poleTideConvert makes the conversion explicit.

g18 = shLowLevel.poleTideConvert(g, From = "IERS2010", To = "IERS2018");
dC21 = g18.C(3, 2) - g.C(3, 2)        % ~1e-10, trend-like in t

% raw triangles, epoch passed explicitly
[C2, S2] = shLowLevel.poleTideConvert(C, S, 2015.5, To = "IERS2018");

Only the (3,2) entries change. Mode= selects "solid", "ocean" or "both" (default); the IERS and Desai coefficients are overridable via SolidCoef/OceanCoef rather than hardcoded. The conversion is exactly antisymmetric — A→A is zero and A→B is the negative of B→A, both pinned by tests.

Check which convention your solutions use before combining releases or centers. The correction is small in absolute terms and almost entirely a trend, which is precisely what makes it dangerous for the quantity most people are after.
