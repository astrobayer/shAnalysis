Filtering

shGaussianWeights

Degree-domain weights for isotropic Gaussian smoothing of Stokes coefficients (Jekeli 1981).

Syntax

Wn = shGaussianWeights(nmax, radius_km)
Wn = shGaussianWeights(nmax, radius_km, 'R', R_km)

Description

Computes the classic Jekeli (1981) / Wahr et al. (1998) Gaussian averaging weights via the stable recursion b = ln(2)/(1-cos(radius/R)); W0=1; W1=(1+e^-2b)/(1-e^-2b) - 1/b; W_(n+1) = -(2n+1)/b W_n + W_(n-1). radius_km is the half-width radius at which the equivalent spatial averaging kernel drops to half its peak value -- larger radius means stronger smoothing (faster high-degree decay of Wn).

Validated (see validate_filters.py) against direct numerical Legendre-polynomial expansion of the continuous spatial-domain Gaussian kernel: max abs difference 2.4e-14 over n=0..60 at 300 km radius.

Input Arguments

ArgumentDescription
nmaxMaximum degree to compute weights for.
radius_kmGaussian averaging radius [km] (e.g. 300 for a typical GRACE mass-change map).
'R'Sphere radius used in b = ln(2)/(1-cos(radius/R)), default 6378.1363 km.

Output Arguments

ArgumentDescription
Wn(nmax+1)x1 vector of weights, Wn(1) = W0 = 1 always.

Examples

Comparing filter radii

Wn300 = shGaussianWeights(60, 300);
Wn500 = shGaussianWeights(60, 500);
plot(0:60, Wn300, 0:60, Wn500); legend('300 km','500 km');
xlabel('degree n'); ylabel('W_n');

A wider radius suppresses high-degree coefficients more aggressively (smaller Wn at high n), trading spatial resolution for noise reduction.
