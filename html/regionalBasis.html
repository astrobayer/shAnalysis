Regional bases for a selectable basin

Local basis functions - radial basis functions or Slepian tapers - for a basin chosen by name, as the state of a regional Kalman filter.

Syntax

[region, info] = shLowLevel.readBasin("Elbe")
B = shLowLevel.regionalBasis(idx, region)
B = shLowLevel.regionalBasis(idx, "Elbe", Type="slepian", Threshold=0.5)
[a, rec] = shLowLevel.regionalProject(Cs, Ss, B)
B = ts.regionalBasis("Elbe");  [a, rec] = ts.regionalProject(B);

Description

Every basis is a linear map to spherical-harmonic coefficients: x = B.T * a, with B.T of size P×K, band-limited at the index's Lmax. That one choice lets the rest of the toolbox stay what it is - an SH solution or normal equation enters the basis as T' N T and T' b, a grid or a point functional as A_SH * T, a model series through the projector B.Proj - and it makes the global SH basis (Type="sh", T = I) the oracle of the regional filter: on it, the regional chain must be the global one. It is the first step towards a regional Kalman filter after Kurtenbach (2012) on local basis functions; the process model, the observation adapters and the filter follow.

The region. shLowLevel.readBasin finds a basin of a GravIS GeoJSON file (the 100 river basins of data/gravis/basins_rivbas.json by default; the Greenland and Antarctic files ship too) by its name or a unique part of it, and returns a wrap-safe region handle: basins that cross lon 0 (Loire, Niger, Volta) and rings around a pole are inside where they should be, which a [lat lon] polygon in [0, 360) is not. Any other region form - a handle, a polygon, a mask - is accepted as well.

Radial basis functions (Type="rbf", the default) follow Eicker (2008) and Woehnke et al. (2025): T(:,k) = phi_n .* Ybar(x_k)', so each function is the Legendre series ∑ phi_n (2n+1) P_n(cos ψ) around its node. The shape coefficients come from the signal's degree amplitudes, phi_n = σ_n / √(2n+1) (Kernel="amplitude", their eq. 7), from Kaula's 1/n² (the default; Woehnke et al. found it within 0.07% in correlation), or are all one ("shannon"). Nodes come from an icosahedral grid of 10 (j+1)² + 2 points or a Reuter grid; the default level makes the global grid as large as the SH basis (j = 30 at nmax 96).

Slepian tapers (Type="slepian") are those of slepianBasis, kept by Shannon number, count or a concentration threshold (Steffen et al. 2026 use 0.5).

The buffer. The region is grown by BufferKm (600 km, Woehnke's computation area: the region plus twice 300 km) before nodes are selected, tapers concentrated and the projector weighted. A node belongs to the basis when its nearest quadrature point is inside the buffered region.

The projector is the least-squares fit over the buffered region, Proj = (T'KT)+ T'K with K = Y' diag(w.*mask) Y; for Slepian tapers it is exactly G', so regionalProject equals slepianProject there. Only grid points inside the buffered region are formed, so the cost follows the region, not the sphere.

Examples

idx = shLowLevel.shIndex(60);
B = shLowLevel.regionalBasis(idx, "Elbe");               % RBF, Kaula kernel
fprintf("%d RBF nodes of %d\n", B.K, B.nGlobal);
S = shLowLevel.regionalBasis(idx, "Elbe", Type="slepian", Threshold=0.5);
[a, rec] = ts.regionalProject(ts.regionalBasis("Elbe"));  % K x T regional state
Validated in tools/dev/validate_regional_basis.py: the icosahedral node count 10 f² + 2, the RBF against scipy's Legendre series (1.2e-14), the projector's normal equations (2e-11) and span reproduction (9e-12), projector = G' for Slepian (2.2e-15), the Elbe's area against its exact spherical polygon (140 031 km²; the mask quadrature is 3.6% below it at nmax 60), the Loire across lon 0 and a ring around the south pole.
