Regional v5.14.0

Regional bases for a selectable basin

Local basis functions - radial basis functions or Slepian tapers - for a basin chosen by name, as the state of a regional Kalman filter.

Syntax

[region, info] = shLowLevel.readBasin("Elbe")
B = shLowLevel.regionalBasis(idx, region)
B = shLowLevel.regionalBasis(idx, "Elbe", Type="slepian", Threshold=0.5)
[a, rec] = shLowLevel.regionalProject(Cs, Ss, B)
B = ts.regionalBasis("Elbe");  [a, rec] = ts.regionalProject(B);
[tsK, out] = ts.regionalKalman(B, ModelSeries=tsm, kn=kn)

Description

Every basis is a linear map to spherical-harmonic coefficients: x = B.T * a, with B.T of size P×K, band-limited at the index's Lmax. That one choice lets the rest of the toolbox stay what it is - an SH solution or normal equation enters the basis as T' N T and T' b, a grid or a point functional as A_SH * T, a model series through the projector B.Proj - and it makes the global SH basis (Type="sh", T = I) the oracle of the regional filter: on it, the regional chain must be the global one. It is the state of a regional Kalman filter after Kurtenbach (2012) on local basis functions; the process model, the observation adapters and the filter itself are described below.

The region. shLowLevel.readBasin finds a basin of a GravIS GeoJSON file (the 100 river basins of data/gravis/basins_rivbas.json by default; the Greenland and Antarctic files ship too) by its name or a unique part of it, and returns a wrap-safe region handle: basins that cross lon 0 (Loire, Niger, Volta) and rings around a pole are inside where they should be, which a [lat lon] polygon in [0, 360) is not. Any other region form - a handle, a polygon, a mask - is accepted as well.

Radial basis functions (Type="rbf", the default) follow Eicker (2008) and Woehnke et al. (2025): T(:,k) = phi_n .* Ybar(x_k)', so each function is the Legendre series ∑ phi_n (2n+1) P_n(cos ψ) around its node. The shape coefficients come from the signal's degree amplitudes, phi_n = σ_n / √(2n+1) (Kernel="amplitude", their eq. 7), from Kaula's 1/n² (the default; Woehnke et al. found it within 0.07% in correlation), or are all one ("shannon"). Nodes come from an icosahedral grid of 10 (j+1)² + 2 points or a Reuter grid; the default level makes the global grid as large as the SH basis (j = 30 at nmax 96).

Slepian tapers (Type="slepian") are those of slepianBasis, kept by Shannon number, count or a concentration threshold (Steffen et al. 2026 use 0.5).

The buffer. The region is grown by BufferKm (600 km, Woehnke's computation area: the region plus twice 300 km) before nodes are selected, tapers concentrated and the projector weighted. A node belongs to the basis when its nearest quadrature point is inside the buffered region.

The projector is the least-squares fit over the buffered region, Proj = (T'KT)+ T'K with K = Y' diag(w.*mask) Y; for Slepian tapers it is exactly G', so regionalProject equals slepianProject there. Only grid points inside the buffered region are formed, so the cost follows the region, not the sphere.

The process model in the basis v5.15.0

shLowLevel.regionalProcessModel (and ts.regionalProcessModel(B)) builds Kurtenbach's (2012) stochastic process model in the basis: the climatology of a geophysical model series is removed, every epoch is projected onto the basis, and the VAR model is estimated by estimateVAR - for order 1 his B = Sigma(1) Sigma(0)^-1, Q = Sigma(0) - B Sigma(1)'. The two steps before the estimate commute, so their order does not matter. On the SH basis the result is bit for bit the model kalmanChain builds. His two adjustments are options: QScale, the α of his eq. 4.8 that scales Q alone (α = 10 cut his largest error RMS from 4.83 to 3.41 cm EWH), and GroundNoise, an isotropic white EWH variance added to Sigma(0), which changes the dynamics as well and needs the load Love numbers. info.representation reports the share of the model's regional signal the basis can carry at all.

B = tsModel.regionalBasis("Elbe", Type="slepian", Threshold=0.5);
[model, info] = tsModel.regionalProcessModel(B, QScale=10);
[info.representation, model.specRadius]

Observations as normal equations in the basis v5.16.0

shLowLevel.regionalNEQ(B, kind, ...) turns one epoch of gravity information of any level into a normal equation in the basis coefficients, N = A' C^-1 A, b = A' C^-1 y, formed through the Cholesky factor of the covariance. "solution" takes an SH coefficient vector and its covariance (g.regionalNEQ(B) does it with the field's formal sigmas); "neq" carries an SH normal equation - a SINEX NEQ, or one built by another toolbox such as a GNSS loading inversion - into the basis as T' N T; "points" takes point functionals in any quantity of kernelFactors at any height - a grid of EWH, gravity disturbances at altitude - and, with Direction, the gradient along a direction, the gravity vector for the potential. Combine forms differences, the shape of a line-of-sight gravity difference. The result is a neqCombine contribution with ltpl and nobs, so several kinds of one epoch combine there with variance components, and its N and b update kalmanFilter.

r1 = g.regionalNEQ(B);                                          % a GRACE month
r2 = shLowLevel.regionalNEQ(B, "points", Values=dg, LatDeg=lat, LonDeg=lon, ...
    Height=450e3, Quantity="gravity_disturbance", Cov=1e-16);
[a, out] = shLowLevel.neqCombine([r1, r2]);                     % VCE of the two

Line-of-sight gravity differences v5.19.0

Kind "lgd" takes the line-of-sight gravity differences of a satellite pair, LGD = e . (gB - gA) with e = (rB - rA)/|rB - rA|, the definition of lgdToolbox. It builds the design from the two orbits: the gradient of the potential along e at B minus that at A. The orbits must be in the Earth-fixed frame of the coefficients; lgdToolbox works in an inertial frame, so the caller rotates both (the LGD is a scalar and does not change). The along-track processing stays in lgdToolbox. Its band filter is passed as Operator and applied once to [Values, A], data first: lgd.spectralOperator fits its edge predictor on the first column and applies the fitted map to every column, so the design is filtered exactly as the data are. Values are therefore the unfiltered LGD, Valid is the mask of the data's own filtered call, and Cov belongs to the filtered data. Rows the operator leaves non-finite and rows outside Valid are dropped together with their part of Cov. One arc becomes one record, which regionalKalman takes in record mode.

Checked against lgdToolbox's own forward models (tools/dev/validate_regional_lgd.py): degrees 0 and 2 with C00 = 1 and C20 = -J2/sqrt(5) reproduce lgdReferenceJ2 to 1.7e-15 |g|, and degree 0 its 50-digit Kepler pair (oracle O2) to 5.3e-16 |g|. Degrees 2..30 agree with Cartesian differences of the potential to the difference floor (1.8e-8). An operator fitted on its first column keeps noise-free data on the design only when it is called once on [y, A].

[lgdF, li] = lgd.fromRangeAcceleration(t, rhoAcc, Method="geometric", ..., Band=band);
op = @(X) lgd.spectralOperator(t, X, Band=band);              % the same operator
rec = shLowLevel.regionalNEQ(B, "lgd", Values=li.components.unfiltered, ...
    PositionA=rA_ecef, PositionB=rB_ecef, Operator=op, Valid=li.valid, Cov=C);

The regional Kalman filter v5.17.0

shLowLevel.regionalKalman(obs, B, ModelSeries=tsm) (and ts.regionalKalman(B, ModelSeries=tsm)) is the single point of access to the chain. It estimates the process model in the basis from the model series, turns every observation epoch into a normal equation in the basis, and runs the existing kalmanFilter in its normal-equation mode and the rtsSmoother - together the joint least-squares adjustment over all epochs (Kvas 2019). The observations are an shSeries with formal sigmas (or NoiseCov), whose climatology is removed first and restored in the basis afterwards, or a struct array of basis normal equations with an epoch field - any kind of regionalNEQ, combined per epoch with neqCombine. An epoch of the Epochs grid without an observation is a gap: prediction only.

The smoothed coefficients leave the chain through linear functionals whose sigmas come from the full smoothed covariance: the SH coefficients T a (the output series, with sigmas through T), the EWH at the region's quadrature points (out.grid) and its area-weighted mean over the unbuffered region (out.basin, mean and sigma). The basin sigma cannot be recovered from the output series afterwards, whose sigmas are per coefficient; this is where it is formed. The EWH outputs need the load Love numbers kn. On the SH basis the chain is kalmanChain: the same process model bit for bit, the same estimates to 5.6e-15.

B = tsGrace.regionalBasis("Elbe", Type="slepian", Threshold=0.5);
[tsK, out] = tsGrace.regionalKalman(B, ModelSeries=tsESM, kn=kn, QScale=10);
errorbar(out.epochs, out.basin.mean, out.basin.sigma)    % basin mean EWH [m]

Remove-compute-restore v5.18.0

A regional basis sees signal from outside its region only through the observations, and what it cannot represent leaks into the coefficients it has. Background = tsb removes a global background series from the observations before they become normal equations in the basis and restores it in every output: the series is b + T (a + Proj c), and out.grid and out.basin carry the background's EWH too. The background is taken as error-free, so the sigmas do not change. With the process model fixed the chain is linear, so a background holding a fraction c of an outside signal leaves exactly 1 - c of its leakage; that identity is the test (5.6e-15). A basis built on an index with Nmin above 2 leaves the low degrees to the background entirely. In record mode the records must already be reduced by the background; it is only restored.

Measured in a closed loop (tools/dev/validate_regional_rcr.py; nmax 30, a cap north of 60°N, a 30 cm seasonal neighbour and a degree-2..4 random walk, five seeds): the outside signal raises the grid RMS over the region by 28%; with an 80% background the error is within 3.6% of what the same data give without any outside signal. Two cautions from the same loop. A background must be a good field: the observations' own unfiltered low degrees as background did worse than none (1.57 against 1.20 cm). And the process model may come from the full model series, not only from the reduced one (worst ratio 1.011). Woehnke et al. (2025) observe EWH on an area 300 km wider than the region; measured here it helps without a background (median 1.04 against 1.20 cm) and costs in every seed with one, so it is not a chain option. Point records (regionalNEQ "points") give it to whoever has gridded data.

[tsK, out] = tsGrace.regionalKalman(B, ModelSeries=tsESM, kn=kn, ...
    Background=tsHydrologyModel);                  % removed, filtered, restored

Examples

idx = shLowLevel.shIndex(60);
B = shLowLevel.regionalBasis(idx, "Elbe");               % RBF, Kaula kernel
fprintf("%d RBF nodes of %d\n", B.K, B.nGlobal);

Validated in tools/dev/validate_regional_basis.py: the icosahedral node count 10 f² + 2, the RBF against scipy's Legendre series (1.2e-14), the projector's normal equations (2e-11) and span reproduction (9e-12), projector = G' for Slepian (2.2e-15), the Elbe's area against its exact spherical polygon (140 031 km²; the mask quadrature is 3.6% below it at nmax 60), the Loire across lon 0 and a ring around the south pole. The filter in tools/dev/validate_regional_kalman.py: on the SH basis the information form equals kalmanChain's gain form (5.6e-15), on an RBF basis filter + smoother equal the joint least-squares adjustment (2.6e-15), and a closed loop gives basin-mean errors of 1.07 predicted sigmas (RMS, 60 seeds). Remove-compute-restore in tools/dev/validate_regional_rcr.py: the restore algebra to 4.8e-14, and the closed loop with signal outside the region. LGD observations in tools/dev/validate_regional_lgd.py, against lgdToolbox's point-mass + J2 and 50-digit Kepler references.
