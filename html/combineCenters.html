Combination

shLowLevel.combineCenters

VCE-weighted multi-center combination with partial redundancies. Two levels exist since v3.20: this page's combineCenters works on the solutions with approximate noise shapes; shLowLevel.neqCombine (below) works on the normal equations before solving — the rigorous COST-G way, when SINEX NEQs are available.

Syntax

[tsComb, info] = shLowLevel.combineCenters({tsITSG, tsCSR, tsGFZ}, Robust=true);
plot(info.epochs, info.weights')      % the weight time series diagnostic
imagesc(info.interCenterCorr)         % the honesty diagnostic

Model

x^(t)   = ( Σ w_c N_c⁻¹ )⁻¹ Σ w_c N_c⁻¹ x_c(t),   w_c = 1/s²_c(t)
s²_c(t) = v_c′ N_c⁻¹ v_c / r_c(t)
r_c(t)  = P − tr( H · w_c N_c⁻¹ )        (partial redundancy)

ONE factor per (center, month) — the COST-G granularity: relative center quality is time-variable and the redundancy (thousands per month at n96) supports it. The partial-redundancy term is essential; without it factors bias low. Noise shapes N_c: each center's own (order, C/S, parity) block covariance from its climatology residuals, TRACE-NORMALIZED to mean unit variance - so the s2 factors carry each center's absolute noise power and are comparable across centers, while the combination weights are invariant under the normalization (v2.4.1). All algebra runs block-wise. Robust=true takes the median over per-block unbiased estimates q_b/r_b (outlier-month protection). Python-validated: known time-varying factors recovered within scatter; the combined field beats every single center (std 0.685 vs best 1.095 in the validation setup).

Two documented limits. (1) Common-mode errors — shared K-band data, AOD1B, ocean tides — are invisible to inter-center VCE; posterior sigmas are a LOWER bound. (2) Inter-center correlations violate the independence assumption; info.interCenterCorr shows how optimistic the weights are. Combine on GSM level BEFORE TN-14/TN-13 (identical for all centers — apply once afterwards).

neqCombine — combination on the normal-equation level (v3.20)

When the contributions come as normal equations (ITSG SINEX, fetchITSGSINEX), combining before solving preserves the full information content of every contribution — the COST-G approach. One Foerstner/Koch variance component per contribution, partial redundancies, and the invariant ∑ri = ∑ni − P asserted at every iteration:

a = shLowLevel.readSINEX(fileA, Index=idx);   % kind='NEQ'; v3.20 also parses
b = shLowLevel.readSINEX(fileB, Index=idx);   % +SOLUTION/STATISTICS (nobs, l'Pl)
[x, out] = shLowLevel.neqCombine([a, b]);
disp(out.sigma2')                             % who carries how much noise power
Two contracts. (1) VCE needs ltpl and nobs on every contribution (the SINEX STATISTICS block); without them it refuses loudly — pass fixed Weights= instead, never silent assumptions. (2) All contributions must share the SAME background models and parametrization; no algebra can detect a background mismatch for you.

Fixed Weights= reproduce the directly stacked weighted least squares exactly (unit-tested); the VCE path recovers known variance factors within statistical scatter (Python pre-validation, tools/dev/validate_neqcombine.py). The common-mode caveat above applies unchanged: shared background models are invisible to inter-contribution VCE on either level.
